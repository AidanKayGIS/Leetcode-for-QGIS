LEETCODE FOR QGIS - CHALLENGE IDEA BACKLOG
=========================================
Compiled: 2026-10-27

Purpose: High-level ideas for map-based, automatically graded QGIS puzzles.
Difficulty labels are provisional. Each challenge should ultimately specify exact
input data, coordinate system, analysis rules, submission format, and tolerances.

PART A - FOUNDATIONAL GIS CHALLENGES
------------------------------------
01. The Coffee Shop Dead Zone [Easy]
    Find the largest residential neighbourhood farther than 500 m from any coffee shop.
    Concepts: buffers, difference, polygon area.

02. The Population Density Trap [Easy]
    Rank districts by population per unit area, not total population.
    Concepts: projections, joins, field calculations.

03. The Border-Crossing River [Easy]
    Find the river that crosses the most administrative boundaries.
    Concepts: intersections, spatial joins, counts.

04. The Grid Detective [Easy]
    Identify the 1 km grid cell containing the most incidents.
    Concepts: fishnet grids, point-in-polygon counting.

05. The Missing Green Space [Easy]
    Find the residential district with the smallest proportion of green space.
    Concepts: overlay, dissolve, areas.

06. The Cholera Detective [Medium; historical]
    Using 1854 London deaths and pump locations, identify which water pump has
    the most deaths assigned by proximity. Proximity does not prove water use.
    Concepts: nearest facility, Voronoi, historical GIS.

07. The Ambulance Race [Medium]
    Identify which of several ambulances reaches an emergency fastest by road.
    Concepts: shortest paths, networks.

08. The Flooded Village [Medium]
    Count buildings intersecting a flood extent and compare with centroid-based counts.
    Concepts: spatial predicates, overlays.

09. The Wind Farm Dilemma [Medium]
    Locate parcels meeting slope, road-distance and dwelling-setback restrictions.
    Concepts: suitability analysis, buffers, raster slope.

10. The Urban Heat Island [Medium]
    Find the neighbourhood with highest mean surface temperature and measure green cover.
    Concepts: raster zonal statistics, land cover.

11. The Perfect Hospital [Hard]
    Pick the candidate hospital site that serves the most people within a 15-minute drive.
    Concepts: isochrones, population-weighted accessibility.

12. The Island Problem [Hard]
    Find settlements disconnected from hospitals after specified bridges close.
    Concepts: graph connectivity, road networks.

13. The Invisible Tower [Hard]
    Site a communications tower to maximise line-of-sight visibility of settlements.
    Concepts: DEM, viewsheds, optimisation.

14. The Evacuation Puzzle [Hard]
    Assign residents to capacity-limited shelters while minimising network travel.
    Concepts: allocation, capacity constraints, optimisation.

15. The Gerrymandering Detector [Hard]
    Rank electoral districts by Polsby-Popper geometric compactness.
    Concepts: area, perimeter, geometry metrics.

16. The Wildlife Corridor [Hard]
    Find the least-cost route between reserves across a landscape resistance raster.
    Concepts: raster cost surfaces, least-cost paths.

17. The Great Divide [Hard]
    Delineate watersheds and find the settlement closest to a drainage divide.
    Concepts: terrain hydrology, watershed boundaries.

18. The Contamination Frontier [Expert]
    Interpolate sampled contamination levels and identify settlements above a threshold.
    Concepts: interpolation, uncertainty, raster sampling.

19. The Facility Location Problem [Expert]
    Place five depots to minimise population-weighted travel cost (p-median).
    Concepts: location-allocation, optimisation.

20. The Landslide Predictor [Expert]
    Combine terrain, geology and rainfall to rank landslide susceptibility.
    Concepts: raster modelling, predictive validation.

21. The Signal Blackout [Expert]
    Find one new transmitter site that covers the largest previously unserved population.
    Concepts: multiple viewsheds, incremental coverage.

22. The Vanishing Forest [Expert]
    Identify the largest contiguous patch of forest loss across two time periods.
    Concepts: remote sensing, change detection, connected components.

23. Find the Fake Island [Special]
    Detect an intentionally introduced coastal geometry or topology error.
    Concepts: validity, topology QA.

24. The Projection Paradox [Special]
    Explain and quantify area differences arising from projection versus geodesic methods.
    Concepts: coordinate reference systems, measurement.

25. The Missing Village [Special]
    Recover a hidden village location from distances to three known landmarks.
    Concepts: trilateration, geometric intersections.

PART B - FAMOUS PAPER / RESEARCH-INSPIRED CHALLENGES
---------------------------------------------------
These can become a special 'Paper Behind the Puzzle' collection.
Some are research-led versions or extensions of Part A entries.

26. The Coastline Paradox [Easy-Medium]
    Measure Britain's coastline at several ruler scales and estimate fractal dimension.
    Inspired by: Mandelbrot (1967), 'How Long Is the Coast of Britain?'
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.156.3775.636

27. John Snow: Walking Distance vs Straight-Line Distance [Medium]
    Revisit the cholera case by comparing pump assignments using road and Euclidean distances.
    Inspired by: Snow (1855), 'On the Mode of Communication of Cholera'.

28. Does Distance Really Matter? [Easy-Medium]
    Compute Moran's I for clustered, random and alternating spatial values.
    Inspired by: Tobler (1970), 'A Computer Movie Simulating Urban Growth
    in the Detroit Region'. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/143141

29. Find the Hidden Spatial Outlier [Medium-Hard]
    Find high-high, low-low and high-low neighbourhoods using local Moran's I.
    Inspired by: Anselin (1995), 'Local Indicators of Spatial Association - LISA'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1538-4632.1995.tb00338.x

30. Save the Last Wildlife Corridor [Expert]
    Compare least-cost paths with circuit-theory connectivity and identify a restoration bottleneck.
    Inspired by: McRae et al. (2008), 'Using Circuit Theory to Model Connectivity
    in Ecology, Evolution, and Conservation'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1890/07-1861.1

31. The Divided City [Hard]
    Simulate local residential preferences and measure emergent spatial segregation.
    Inspired by: Schelling (1971), 'Dynamic Models of Segregation'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/0022250X.1971.9989794

32. The Shopping Centre Wars [Medium]
    Use a Huff gravity model to choose the site attracting the most expected customers.
    Inspired by: Huff (1963), 'A Probabilistic Analysis of Shopping Center Trade Areas'.

33. Rebuild the River Network [Hard]
    Extract streams and upstream drainage from a DEM using a defined flow model.
    Inspired by: O'Callaghan and Mark (1984),
    'The Extraction of Drainage Networks from Digital Elevation Data'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0734-189X(84)80011-0

34. The Most Accessible Place in the City [Medium]
    Calculate Hansen-style accessibility and find the neighbourhood with best access to jobs.
    Inspired by: Hansen (1959), 'How Accessibility Shapes Land Use'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01944365908978307

35. The Relationship That Changes Across Space [Expert]
    Use geographically weighted regression to find where a local relationship reverses.
    Inspired by: Brunsdon, Fotheringham and Charlton (1996),
    'Geographically Weighted Regression: A Method for Exploring Spatial Nonstationarity'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1538-4632.1996.tb00936.x

36. The Hotspot Illusion [Hard]
    Distinguish isolated high counts from statistically significant local hotspots.
    Inspired by: Getis and Ord (1992),
    'The Analysis of Spatial Association by Use of Distance Statistics'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1538-4632.1992.tb00261.x

37. The Road That Changes Everything [Hard]
    Pick the proposed road connection that most reduces population-weighted hospital travel time.
    Inspired by: Dijkstra (1959), 'A Note on Two Problems in Connexion with Graphs'.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386390

IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
--------------------
- Prefer frozen, versioned downloadable datasets (ideally GeoPackage).
- Specify CRS, geometry validity, distance convention, raster resolution,
  NoData handling, thresholds and boundary inclusion rules.
- Accept feature IDs, scalar values or spatial files as appropriate.
- Precompute canonical answers with tolerances and hidden checks.
- Allow multiple correct QGIS workflows; grade outputs, not click sequences.
- Cite scientific inspirations accurately; adapted puzzles are not direct
  replications of published results unless source data and methods match.
- Useful data leads: Natural Earth, OpenStreetMap/Geofabrik, GeoDa Snow data.
