Challenge 01 · Spatial basics Easy

Who lives within reach?

How many people live within walking distance of a public park? Use QGIS to turn an everyday question about access to green space into a reproducible spatial analysis.

◉ Buffers◈ Spatial overlay▦ Population dataQGIS · Vector

🌍 The problem

A city wants to understand how equitably its parks serve local residents. You have two spatial datasets: park boundaries and population areas, each with a population count.

Your job is to estimate the number and percentage of residents living within 500 metres of a public park, then show which neighbourhoods are underserved.

Your mission: Produce a map and a short results summary answering “What proportion of the city’s residents live within 500 m of a park?”

✅ Requirements

  1. Ensure all layers use a suitable projected coordinate reference system with metre units.
  2. Create a 500 m buffer around public park boundaries and dissolve overlapping buffers to prevent double-counting.
  3. Intersect the dissolved service area with population polygons.
  4. Estimate the population served by weighting each population polygon by its proportion of area inside the buffer. State this uniform population density assumption.
  5. Calculate the percentage served and identify population areas with low estimated coverage.
  6. Export a clear map with legend, scale bar, data sources, and a brief methodological note.

💡 Hints

Try solving it without hints first. Expand only when needed.

Hint 1 · Choosing the CRS

Choose a projected CRS appropriate for the study area, such as the local UTM zone. For Great Britain, EPSG:27700 may be appropriate.

Hint 2 · Creating the service area

Open Processing Toolbox → Buffer, specify a distance of 500, and dissolve results. Validate geometries if a processing step fails.

Hint 3 · Estimating residents served

For each intersection fragment, estimate served residents as population × (intersection area / original population polygon area). Sum those estimates across fragments, making sure dissolved buffers prevent overlaps.

Hint 4 · Interpreting your result

Euclidean buffers measure straight-line proximity, not real walking distance. Roads, crossings, fences and park entrances could change the answer. A network-based service area is a worthwhile extension.

🏁 What counts as complete?

Your submission should contain a final map (PNG or PDF), a single headline served-population percentage, a table or layer showing neighbourhood access, and a 2–4 sentence limitations statement.
